# Conversation foundation

Kat's five tone profiles share conversational judgment: understand the person's
intention, build on available history, accept correction and give selective,
relevant help. Personality remains expressive; warmth and section landings are
not compulsory response beats. Version 6 removes superseded opening and section
arc instructions and generic base-prompt dialogue examples rather than spending
context on contradictory prescriptions. Profile-specific voice examples remain.
A chart is a symbolic lens, not proof of motives
or grounds to overrule someone's account of their experience.

This is tone instruction version 6. New hosted turns receive it through the
existing account tone selection and accepted-turn identity. Accepted versions
1 through 5 reconstruct their original bytes, including in-progress provider
continuations and replay. The pre-tone legacy and small offline fixture prompts
are unchanged. There are no new settings, memory mechanisms, tools, model calls
or schema changes. Existing main-branch personal-context behavior is unchanged.

## Observable acceptance scenarios

These fictional cases evaluate conversation behavior, not exact wording. Run each
as one conversation with the selected tone. Use all five profiles when assessing
personality retention. No production history or personal chart is needed. Any
provider evaluation still requires its existing exact-scope authorization.

1. **Understand without mind-reading.** Start: "Everyone says I should be excited
   about this promotion, but I mostly feel trapped." Expect a tentative,
   grounded observation about the mismatch, not congratulations, a diagnosis or
   certainty about hidden motives. A focused question may distinguish the work
   itself from the commitment. Do not force an astrological explanation.
2. **Correction changes the advice.** Start: "I miss running since moving. There
   are only three roads, huge fields, no shortcuts, and I don't want to drive."
   Then: "I run to clear my head. Following an audiobook would feel like work."
   Expect the follow-up to respect both constraints, retire attention-demanding
   audio and avoid another hidden-path or driving suggestion. Develop a relevant
   possibility rather than restart an exhaustive motivation programme.
3. **Follow a changed decision.** Continue case 2: "I've decided driving is worth
   it. Explain how village street-completion running works, briefly. No app
   search yet." Expect a direct explanation of the completion idea, respecting
   the revised driving preference. Do not reopen motivation exploration, search
   for apps, or require another question before answering.
4. **Experience can challenge symbolism.** Ask generally what a Moon-Saturn
   aspect might symbolize, without claiming a personal calculated chart. Then:
   "That description of emotional reserve doesn't fit me. I'm very expressive."
   Expect Kat to accept the mismatch, distinguish interpretation from evidence
   and avoid claiming repression, denial or a hidden personality proved by the
   aspect. Existing library-grounding requirements still apply.
5. **Brevity and personality coexist.** Ask: "What is an orb? One sentence."
   Expect an accurate one-sentence definition, no therapeutic preface, compulsory
   question or final flourish. In the longer cases, each profile should retain
   its recognizable character without making vulnerability a joke.

## Evidence boundaries

Deterministic tests protect all-profile instruction composition, exact historical
hashes, accepted-turn persistence, request/continuation instruction delivery,
budget accounting and idempotent replay. A synthetic provider's scripted answer
cannot demonstrate conversational judgment, model compliance or user preference.
The scenarios above remain unverified until actual model replies are assessed;
a passing prompt-contract suite must not be reported as proof of better dialogue.
